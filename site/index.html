Scope and use

ThinFilmBook links thermodynamic driving forces, surface kinetics, deposition routes, epitaxy, interface evolution, stress, characterization, and reliability. It focuses on transferable physical reasoning and clearly bounded models rather than material-specific process recipes. Readers are assumed to know introductory thermodynamics, materials science, chemistry, and semiconductor processing.

The numerical examples and interactive labs are educational illustrations. Their assumptions, units, and omitted effects are stated beside each model. None of the outputs are calibrated recipes, manufacturing setpoints, or evidence of fab qualification.

All chapters

Film functions and thermodynamic drivers

Translate a layer's role into measurable targets; distinguish favorable states from the kinetic route that can reach them.

  1. 01What a Film Must DoA thin film is designed as a functional layer in a larger stack. This chapter translates that role into measurable targets for thickness, composition, uniformity, interfaces, and reliability.
  2. 02Thermodynamic Drivers of Film GrowthThis chapter uses chemical potential and surface and interface energies to describe why deposition can favor a film, while separating thermodynamic favorability from the kinetic path that growth actually follows.

Surface kinetics and nucleation

Track arrival, adsorption, diffusion, desorption, nucleation, island growth, and coalescence.

  1. 03Surface Kinetics during DepositionArrival flux, sticking, residence, desorption, and surface diffusion determine how incoming species become incorporated material. The chapter connects these processes qualitatively to temperature, pressure, and flux without proposing fab recipes.
  2. 04From Nuclei to a Connected FilmFilms often develop through incubation, nucleation, island growth, and coalescence. This chapter introduces idealized growth modes and explains why continuity and percolation depend on material, surface, and measurement scale.

Deposition methods and transport

Compare PVD, CVD, and ALD through their mechanisms, transport limits, and stated assumptions.

  1. 05Physical Vapor Deposition: Evaporation and SputteringCompares evaporation and sputtering through source physics, transport, reactive operation, and the energetic conditions that shape deposited-film microstructure.
  2. 06Chemical Vapor Deposition: Transport and ReactionBuilds a quantitative picture of precursor delivery, gas and surface reactions, transport limits, and wafer-scale nonuniformity in CVD.
  3. 07Atomic Layer Deposition: Cycles and ConformalityExplains ALD as sequential, saturating surface chemistry and examines dose, purge, growth per cycle, nucleation, temperature, and feature-scale transport.

Epitaxy and crystalline accommodation

Relate lattice mismatch and strain to relaxation, defects, and system-dependent critical-thickness estimates.

  1. 08Epitaxy: Registry, Strain, and DefectsIntroduces crystal registry across an interface, lattice-mismatch strain, and the limits of critical-thickness descriptions for misfit-dislocation formation.

Interfaces, diffusion, and stress

Examine intermixing, diffusion length, adhesion, curvature, and thin-film stress with explicit model boundaries.

  1. 09Interfaces, Interdiffusion, and Phase DevelopmentConnects interfacial chemistry and morphology to composition profiles, diffusion, and phase development while separating operational measurement depth from bulk material behavior.
  2. 10Film Stress, Adhesion, and DelaminationSeparates intrinsic and thermal stress, explains mismatch-driven loading and fracture pathways, and derives the Stoney stress relation with its assumptions and sign-convention limits.

Metrology, integration, and reliability

Match measurements to claims and connect film properties to process integration, uncertainty, and reliability.

  1. 11Thin-Film Metrology and Measurement ModelsSurveys how thickness, density, roughness, composition, phase, stress, and electrical properties are measured, emphasizing model dependence, uncertainty, and traceability.
  2. 12From Material Targets to Process IntegrationProvides a framework for translating device function into measurable film and interface targets, comparing process families, defining a bounded process window, and managing integration evidence and reliability risks.
