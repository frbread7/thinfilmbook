Chapter 11 · Metrology, integration, and reliability

Thin-Film Metrology and Measurement Models

Surveys how thickness, density, roughness, composition, phase, stress, and electrical properties are measured, emphasizing model dependence, uncertainty, and traceability.

Learning objectives

  • Match common thin-film methods to the quantity and sampling depth they actually measure.
  • Explain why fitted results depend on physical models and sample structure.
  • Identify method-specific limits of XRR, HRXRD, XPS, ellipsometry, and four-point probes.
  • Report uncertainty, calibration, and traceability with measurement results.

Define the measurand before choosing an instrument

“Film thickness” can mean a geometric step height, a fitted optical thickness, an X-ray density-layer thickness [r09], or a mass-per-area equivalent divided by assumed density. These quantities coincide only under suitable uniformity and model assumptions. Roughness, porosity, interfacial layers, lateral nonuniformity, and substrate contributions can change the relation between signal and reported thickness. Define the measurand and the region represented before comparing methods.

Every measurement samples a finite depth, area, and population of structures. A local cross-section may reveal a specific interface while missing wafer-scale variation; an area-averaged optical or X-ray result can conceal local defects. State measurement location, area, sampling plan, and whether the reported value is local, averaged, or inferred. Use standards or reference materials where available, while checking that their composition and geometry are relevant to the sample. [r10]

Thickness, density, and roughness by reflectometry and optics

X-ray reflectivity (XRR) records reflected intensity versus angle or momentum transfer. Interference fringes constrain layer thickness, while critical-angle behavior and fringe envelope help constrain electron density and roughness in a chosen stack model. Several combinations of thickness, density, roughness, and interfacial grading may fit limited or noisy data similarly. Report the stack model, fitting range, parameter correlations, and uncertainty; XRR does not directly provide a unique real-space image. [r09]

Ellipsometry measures polarization changes and infers optical constants and thickness through a model of layer sequence and optical response. Results can be correlated: a thickness estimate may change if refractive index, anisotropy, absorption, or surface roughness is fixed incorrectly. Neither ellipsometry nor XRR alone guarantees lateral uniformity or separates every interfacial feature. Validate the model with independent thickness or composition evidence when the distinction affects a decision.

Composition and structure by spectroscopy and diffraction

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) estimates near-surface elemental composition and chemical states from photoelectron energies and intensities. Quantification depends on sensitivity factors, background and peak models, attenuation, surface contamination, and sample charging. Conventional XPS is surface sensitive; sputter depth profiling can introduce preferential sputtering, mixing, reduction, or other chemical change. State acquisition and preparation conditions and avoid treating a fitted surface ratio as bulk stoichiometry.

High-resolution X-ray diffraction (HRXRD) identifies and quantifies ordered crystalline contributions through peak position, shape, and intensity under an appropriate structural model. Amorphous or poorly ordered fractions may contribute little or diffuse intensity, and overlapping phases, texture, strain, finite domain size, and instrument resolution affect interpretation. A diffraction peak position alone does not uniquely determine composition or strain; complementary composition and structural measurements may be needed.

Stress and electrical properties; uncertainty practice

Curvature methods infer film stress from substrate bending under a mechanical model; they measure curvature first, and inferred stress inherits thickness, elastic-constant, substrate, and sign-convention assumptions. A four-point probe measures voltage and current geometry to estimate sheet resistance; converting to resistivity requires an appropriate thickness and correction for finite sample size, contacts, anisotropy, and nonuniformity. Sheet resistance does not identify the physical cause of a change, such as carrier density, mobility, phase, or thickness. [r08]

A measurement result should include calibration status, reference standards, instrument settings, data-reduction model, repeatability, sampling location, and uncertainty appropriate to the decision. Separate repeatability from systematic effects and record sample history. Traceability means a documented relation through calibrations to recognized references with stated uncertainty; it does not guarantee that the selected model represents the sample correctly. Thin-film reference guide materials can support comparisons when their certified property and use conditions match the task. [r10]

Example

Illustrative four-point-probe conversion: if a hypothetical uniform film has measured sheet resistance R_s=20 Ω/□ and an independently determined thickness t=100 nm, the simple isotropic estimate is ρ=R_s t=(20 Ω)(100×10⁻⁹ m)=2×10⁻⁶ Ω·m. This calculation assumes uniform thickness and conductivity and appropriate probe corrections. It does not establish whether a real specimen meets those assumptions or explain the physical origin of its resistance.

Limitations

  • Instrument outputs are not interchangeable measurands; fitted values depend on the selected physical model.
  • Method-specific depth, area, and preparation effects can bias apparent composition or structure.
  • Traceable calibration does not eliminate model error or guarantee sample representativeness.

Review questions

  1. Why can two methods report different thickness values for the same film without direct contradiction?
  2. For each of XPS, HRXRD, and ellipsometry, give one key model or sampling limitation.
  3. What does metrological traceability establish, and what does it not establish about a fitted thin-film result?
