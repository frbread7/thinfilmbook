ThinFilmBook · References

Technical references

The following public sources informed original explanations and model assumptions. Citations point to the source record; no publisher text, tables, or figures are reproduced here.

IOP Publishing · 1984

J. A. Venables, G. D. T. Spiller, and M. Hanbücken. “Nucleation and growth of thin films.”

Reports on Progress in Physics, 47(4), 399–459.

Review of nucleation, growth modes, and thin-film formation. Used as a technical reference; no prose or figures are reproduced.

https://doi.org/10.1088/0034-4885/47/4/002

American Chemical Society · 2010

Steven M. George. “Atomic Layer Deposition: An Overview.”

Chemical Reviews, 110(1), 111–131.

Overview of ALD principles and process characteristics. Cited for background, not as a source of copied description or process recipes.

https://doi.org/10.1021/cr900056b

AIP Publishing · 2005

Riikka L. Puurunen. “Surface chemistry of atomic layer deposition: A case study for the trimethylaluminum/water process.”

Journal of Applied Physics, 97(12), 121301.

Surface-chemistry analysis for the TMA/water case. Chemistry-specific conclusions are not generalized to other precursor systems.

https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1940727

AVS · 1986

John A. Thornton. “The microstructure of sputter-deposited coatings.”

Journal of Vacuum Science & Technology A, 4(6), 3059–3065.

Reference on structure development in sputter-deposited coatings; regime maps are treated as qualitative heuristics with stated limits.

https://doi.org/10.1116/1.573628

Elsevier · 1991

M. J. G. V. M. Kleijn. “On the modelling of transport phenomena in chemical vapour deposition and its use in reactor design and process optimization.”

Thin Solid Films, 206(1–2), 47–53.

CVD transport modeling reference. This book's resistance model is a deliberately reduced illustration, not a reactor design model.

https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-6090(91)90391-A

Cambridge University Press · 2003

L. B. Freund and S. Suresh. “Thin Film Materials: Stress, Defect Formation and Surface Evolution.”

Cambridge University Press, Cambridge Studies in Mechanics.

Book-length treatment of thin-film stress, defects, and surface evolution. Used for technical grounding, not reproduced content.

https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511754715

Elsevier · 1974

J. W. Matthews and A. E. Blakeslee. “Defects in epitaxial multilayers: I. Misfit dislocations.”

Journal of Crystal Growth, 27, 118–125.

Foundational misfit-dislocation analysis; critical-thickness predictions require system-specific assumptions.

https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0248(74)90424-2

Elsevier · 2009

B. J. Janssen, M. M. Abdalla, F. van Keulen, B. R. Pujada, and G. Q. Zhang. “Celebrating the 100th anniversary of the Stoney equation for film stress: Developments from its implementation in microelectronics to nowadays applications in MEMS research.”

Thin Solid Films, 517(6), 1858–1867.

Review of Stoney-equation applications and limitations. The lab uses only its isotropic, thin-film approximation.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsf.2008.07.014

NIST · 2010

National Institute of Standards and Technology. “Thin-Film X-Ray Metrology for Microelectronics.”

NIST Interagency/Internal Report 7604, NISTIR 7604.

Metrology report on thin-film X-ray measurements and traceability. The book links to the report but does not reproduce its figures or tables.

https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir7604.pdf

NIST · 2026

National Institute of Standards and Technology. “Thin Film RGTMs.”

NIST project page, Project overview; created March 10, 2026; updated September 17, 2026; accessed October 15, 2026.

NIST expands RGTMs as Research Grade Test Materials and describes thin-film materials for characterization inter-comparison. Accessed October 15, 2026; the page remains subject to change.

https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/thin-film-rgtms
